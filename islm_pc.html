How to read
Top-left: Keynesian cross. A lower policy rate raises investment, shifts demand Z up, and increases output.

Bottom-left: IS-LM. The central bank chooses the real rate, so LM is flat.

Bottom-right: Money market. Outside the deflation spiral, the central bank shifts M/P so the equilibrium real rate equals the chosen policy rate. At the ZLB, the nominal rate is fixed at zero and deflation raises the real rate.

Curve logic: Lower r̄ raises investment and shifts demand in the goods market. Higher output shifts liquidity demand to the right. In normal times the money stock adjusts to deliver r̄; in the deflation spiral, the nominal policy rate cannot fall further.

How to read
Top-left: the labor market. Excess inflation moves WS because $W/P \approx [1-(\pi-\pi^e)]F(u,z)$. Supply or markup shocks move PS downward.

Bottom-left: the Phillips curve. A WS shift moves the economy along the PC. A PS shift raises the natural unemployment rate and shifts the PC to the right.

Mapping: Demand shocks mainly change excess inflation and move WS. Supply or markup shocks change price setting and therefore shift natural unemployment and the whole PC.
